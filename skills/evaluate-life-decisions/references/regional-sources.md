# Regional Source Routing

Identify the user's relevant country or region before researching local conditions. Use sources in the local language when they are more authoritative, then explain the finding in the user's language. Select only sources relevant to the decision.

## Global baselines

| Topic | Useful primary or institutional starting points | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Population, development, labor | World Bank Data, ILOSTAT, UN Data, OECD Data Explorer where applicable | country coverage, survey year, harmonized versus national definitions |
| Education and research | UNESCO Institute for Statistics, OECD Education, national education ministries, official university program pages | degree level, field, cohort, domestic/international status |
| Macroeconomy | IMF World Economic Outlook, World Bank, national statistics and central banks | actual versus forecast, real versus nominal, revision date |
| Migration and work authorization | responsible immigration authority and the destination country's current law or guidance | nationality, route, employer, validity date, transitional rules |
| Jobs and occupations | national labor statistics, public employment services, original employer postings | occupation code, location, experience level, salary basis, posting date |
| AI and technology | Stanford AI Index and its underlying data, OECD.AI, original benchmark papers, company documentation, job postings | benchmark definition, model version, deployment evidence, affected tasks versus whole jobs |
| Health | WHO plus the responsible national health authority and relevant clinical guidance | population, evidence grade, current guideline date |

Global datasets help comparison but may lag or smooth local changes. Use national or subnational data for a local decision when definitions and quality are adequate.

## Historical source selection

Search across the region relevant to the user and at least one other context when history is useful. Prefer cases with comparable institutions and technology over cases that merely share a dramatic narrative. Do not imply that a national history determines an individual's personality or choice.
