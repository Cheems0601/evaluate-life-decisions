# Evidence Standard

## Source hierarchy

Use the strongest accessible source appropriate to the claim:

1. law, regulation, official statistics, administrative data, original program or job requirements;
2. original research with methods and data, systematic reviews, or reputable institutional datasets;
3. transparent industry or civil society reports with disclosed methods;
4. high quality reporting that links or names its underlying evidence;
5. books and scholarly histories checked in the relevant edition;
6. commentary, summaries, and search snippets only as discovery leads.

Authority alone is insufficient. Check definitions, incentives, methods, sample coverage, and whether the data answer the user's question.

## Current claims

For each decision critical claim, record:

- publication date and period measured;
- geography and population;
- metric definition and denominator;
- whether it describes a policy goal, observed behavior, or forecast;
- major exclusions or uncertainty;
- an independent corroborating source when practical.

Prefer a national or local primary source for local facts and a comparable international source for context. Do not combine incompatible definitions into a trend.

## Historical cases

Search by mechanism before searching by famous person. Build a case record:

`mechanism | place and institutions | what was knowable at the time | actual options | action | outcome | visible and hidden costs | primary or scholarly sources | similarities | differences | source disputes`

Use primary material for what was said or recorded and reputable scholarship for context and interpretation. A memoir is evidence of the author's account, not automatic proof of events or motives. Later success does not show that the initial decision was rational.

Quote only text that has been directly accessed and verified. Keep quotations short. If the relevant source is unavailable, paraphrase only the points supported by an accessible source or omit the claim.

## Three layer writing

Label the reasoning internally and make the boundaries visible in the answer:

- **Evidence:** what a checked source establishes.
- **Interpretation:** the proposed mechanism or meaning.
- **Analogy:** how that mechanism might transfer to the user's situation.

State the strongest material difference that could break an analogy.

## Conflicts and gaps

When credible sources disagree, explain whether they use different time periods, populations, definitions, or causal models. Do not average incompatible claims. If no reliable evidence is available, name the gap and reduce confidence.
